Rosey: privacy policy

Last updated: October 11, 2026

Rosey is a personal assistant app built by Daniel Davis for his own use. It is not offered to the public. This page explains what the app does with data.

Microphone

While "Hey Rosey" is turned on, the app listens on the phone for its wake phrase. That listening happens entirely on the device using an on-device speech model. Audio is not recorded, stored or sent anywhere until the wake phrase is heard or the user opens a conversation.

During a conversation, the user's voice is streamed to ElevenLabs, the voice service that powers the conversation, and the text of the conversation is saved to the owner's private server.

Personal information

When the user asks about their own information (for example reminders, to-dos, calendar, messages or email), the request is sent to the owner's private server, which is reachable only over the owner's private network. Answers that include personal information require the phone to be unlocked.

Notifications

The app shows notifications about items that need the user. Notification pings delivered through Google's push service carry no message content; the app fetches the details from the owner's private server.

What is not done

  • No data is sold or shared with advertisers or data brokers.
  • No advertising and no analytics or tracking SDKs.
  • Data is sent only to the owner's private server and to ElevenLabs during conversations, always encrypted in transit (TLS).

Keeping and deleting data

Conversation transcripts are kept on the owner's private server and can be deleted by the owner at any time. Uninstalling the app removes everything stored on the phone.
